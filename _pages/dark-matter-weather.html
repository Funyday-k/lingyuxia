1Where does the wind come from?

Fig. 1 · Eq. (8)

The Solar System moves toward Cygnus, so incoming DM travels from that direction. As Earth rotates, the detector moves along its latitude circle. Θ is measured at Earth's center, between the detector radius rdet and the direction toward Cygnus. Near 0° DM arrives from overhead; near 180° it crosses nearly the whole planet.

Wind direction taken as δ ≈ +42° (toward Cygnus), as in the paper. Orbital velocity fixed, so Θ repeats every sidereal day.

The simulation samples the full isoangle range, 0°–180°, in 18 bins of 10°. Density uses every angle bin; the speed plot compares only three representative directions, near 0°, 90° and 180°. Change σχn to compare weak and strong scattering.

3Two knobs: σ̄e sets the rate, σχn sets the shape

Fig. 6 · Fig. 8 · Eq. (11)

Left: signal rate versus Θ on a log scale, so a pure rescaling moves the curve without changing its shape. Right: the resulting daily forecast at one lab, normalized to its mean.
